## Grade: FINITE_STATE_SUFFICES

The claim under test (Sainburg, Theilman, Thielk and Gentner 2019, Nat Commun 10:3636): mutual
information (MI) between syllables of birdsong decays as an exponential plus a power law (the
"composite" model), and the power-law part shows structure beyond Markov dynamics, consistent
with hierarchy. On the Bengalese finch song repository that the authors list as their Bengalese
finch source (Nicholson, Queen and Sober, figshare 4805749 v9, CC BY 4.0), **the composite verdict
fires, and it fires just as strongly on song generated by each bird's own fitted finite-memory
syntax with bouts drawn independently.** On this species, the composite verdict gives no
evidence of structure beyond an 8-syllable memory.

| S4 | every bout a Lin-Tegmark recursive tree (positive control) | 1 of 12 | 12 of 12 |

T4c is calibrated by the registered rule (at most 12.5% EXCEEDS in S0 and in S1: 0.0 and 0.0833).
Two side results matter for reading the published method:

- **The composite verdict fired in 11 of 12 phrase-machine worlds, which are finite-state.** Song
  made of repeated fixed phrases is enough to produce "exponential plus power law".
- **It fired in only 1 of 12 worlds with a planted recursive hierarchy,** while T4c flagged all 12.
  In this design each recursive bout is short (32 syllables, depth 5) and bouts are independent,
  so this says the composite verdict is not a detector of within-bout hierarchy of this size; it
  does not say what it does for deeper hierarchies.

S3 shows that T4c would have flagged song whose bouts carry finite-state persistence (12 of 12);
that is why the registered grade names a finite-memory model with independent bouts, not
"no structure".

## What this means

For Bengalese finches, one of the four species behind the 2019 claim, the shape of the MI decay
curve, including its power-law tail, is what each bird's own finite-memory syntax (memory 2 to 8
syllables, matching the roughly 8-syllable context found by Morita et al. 2021, PLoS Comput Biol
17:e1009707) already produces when bouts are joined by day. The curve shape is therefore not
evidence for hierarchy in this species. This does not show that Bengalese finch song has no
hierarchy; it shows that this statistic cannot see past finite-memory syntax here. The 2019
paper's Markov controls were published chains run as single long sequences, not models fitted
to each bird with the paper's pooling of days and joining of bouts.

## Limits

One species of four; Cassin's vireo (stage 5), house finch (stage 2), chimpanzee actions (stage
3) and speech (stage 6) are registered and queued. Bout order within a day is taken from file
names. A k-gram up to 8 is one finite-state class; for one bird the chosen k is at the cap. The
self-check uses 12 worlds per kind, so its rates are coarse. Prior work searched 2026-11-24 and
again 2026-11-26 (web search; queries on Bengalese finch MI decay with Markov or k-gram
surrogates, and on challenges to Sainburg et al. 2019): no published test of this verdict against
per-bird fitted finite-state surrogates was found. Closest: Morita et al. 2021 (context length by
neural networks), Nakaishi et al. (arXiv 2505.04984, power law and hierarchy in language), and
Katahira et al. 2011 and Jin and Kozhevnikov 2011 (hidden-state Markov models of this species'
syntax, which did not test the MI verdict).
